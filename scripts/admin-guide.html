Chapter 1

Getting started — your first login

Your website has a public side (what visitors see) and an admin side (where you manage everything). The admin side is protected by a login screen so only you can make changes. To reach it, open your website URL and add ?view=admin at the end of the address. For example, if your site lives at https://ahmadhomeworks.sg, go to https://ahmadhomeworks.sg/?view=admin.

You'll see a clean login card asking for your email and password. The demo credentials are admin@homeworks.sg and password admin123. Once you log in for the first time, change this password immediately — see Chapter 14 for how. After you sign in, you'll land on the admin dashboard.

Tip — bookmark the admin URL

Save the admin URL in your browser bookmarks so you don't have to type ?view=admin every time. You can also reach the admin login from the mobile menu — tap the menu icon (three lines) in the top right, then tap "Admin Login".

Chapter 2

The admin dashboard tour

The admin panel has a sidebar (on desktop) or a top bar (on mobile) with five sections. Each one does one job, so you always know where to look. Here's what each one is for:

Overview
Quick stats + recent posts + quick-action buttons
Posts
Create / edit / delete portfolio jobs and blog-style posts
Testimonials
Add / edit / delete client reviews shown on the homepage
Analytics
See site views, post views, WhatsApp clicks, popular posts

The sidebar also has two buttons at the bottom: "View public site" (opens the site as visitors see it) and "Sign out" (always click this when you're done on a shared computer).

Chapter 3

Creating a new post (portfolio job)

A "post" is one job or one before/after showcase on your site. Every post has a title, a category (Plumbing, Painting, etc.), an optional excerpt, optional markdown content, and one or more images. Posts with both a "before" and an "after" image will automatically show a slider on the Before & After page — see Chapter 4 for how to mark them.

1

Open the Posts tab

Click Posts in the sidebar. You'll see a list of every post on your site, with tabs to filter by All / Portfolio / Blog. Click the blue "New post" button in the top right.

2

Fill in the title

Type a clear, descriptive title — e.g. "HDB Kitchen Sink Replacement, Toa Payoh". This title appears on cards, in the detail modal, and in search results. Keep it under 80 characters for best display.

3

Pick type and category

Choose Portfolio Job (most common — shows up in the Portfolio and Before & After sections) or Blog Post (text-only articles, hidden from public nav but still accessible). Then pick a category from the dropdown — these match your services list and can be edited in Site Settings.

4

Add an excerpt (optional but recommended)

A one-line summary shown on cards. Example: "Replaced corroded galvanised pipes with PPR, fixed a stubborn leak under the sink." Keep it punchy — visitors decide whether to click based on this line.

5

Write the content (optional)

The big text area supports Markdown. Use # Heading, ## Sub-heading, - bullet, **bold**. This becomes the body of the post detail page when a visitor clicks in.

6

Upload images

Scroll to the Images section. Drag your photos into the upload box (or click to browse). They're automatically compressed to under 1.5 MB and converted to WebP for fast loading. See Chapter 4 for marking before/after.

7

Toggle Featured / Published

Switch Featured on if you want this post to appear on the homepage "Selected work" section. Switch Published on to make it visible to the public (leave off to keep as a draft).

8

Click Save

Hit the blue Save button at the bottom right. You'll see a success toast and the page will reload, showing your new post in the list.

Chapter 4

Uploading images & marking before/after

Every post can have multiple images. By default, an uploaded image is tagged as gallery — it just shows as a single photo. To enable the before/after slider, you must tag one image as before and another as after. The system will then automatically show the draggable comparison slider on the post detail page and on the Before & After gallery page.

If a post has only a "before" image (or only an "after"), it will simply show as a single photo on the Before & After page — nothing breaks. The slider only appears when both before AND after are present.

Three image kinds — pick one per image

Gallery (default) — single photo, no slider.
Before — the "before work started" photo. Only one per post.
After — the "after work finished" photo. Only one per post.

1

Upload your images

In the post editor's Images section, drag in your before photo and your after photo. Both will appear as thumbnails in a grid, each tagged "G" (gallery) by default.

2

Click the "B" button on the before photo

Hover over the thumbnail of the before photo. You'll see three small buttons in the top-left corner: G (gallery), B (before), A (after). Click B. The thumbnail's badge turns red and shows a checkmark.

3

Click the "A" button on the after photo

Repeat for the after photo — click A. The badge turns green. You'll see a confirmation banner: "Before/After slider will be enabled on this post".

4

Reorder if needed

Use the small arrow buttons at the bottom of each thumbnail to move images left or right. The first image becomes the cover image (shown on cards) unless you override it in the "Cover image URL" field below.

5

Save the post

Hit Save. Visit the Before & After page on the public site — your new post will appear as a card with the slider front and centre. Drag the divider to compare.

Image compression — what you should know

Every image you upload is automatically compressed in your browser before it reaches the server. Files are shrunk to under 1.5 MB and resized to fit within 1920×1920 pixels. PNGs are converted to WebP for smaller file size. You don't need to do anything — just upload the original photo from your phone or camera. The compression preserves more than enough quality for web display.

Chapter 5

Editing an existing post

To change anything about a post you've already created — the title, the text, the images, the featured flag, anything — open it in the editor, make your change, and save. The public site updates the moment you click Save.

1

Go to Posts and find the post

Click Posts in the sidebar. Use the search box (top right) or the All/Portfolio/Blog tabs to filter. Click anywhere on the post's row — the editor opens in a modal.

2

Make your changes

Edit any field — title, excerpt, content, category, images, toggles. The changes are saved only when you click Save, so you can experiment freely.

3

Click Save

Hit the blue Save button at the bottom right. The page reloads and your changes are live on the public site immediately.

Chapter 6

Featuring, unfeaturing & publishing

Two switches at the bottom of the post editor control where a post shows up on your site. Both can be toggled on or off at any time — changes apply the moment you save.

Featured (on)
Shows on the homepage "Selected work" section
Featured (off)
Only shows in Portfolio and Before & After listings
Published (on)
Visible to the public
Published (off)
Hidden from public — saved as a draft only you can see

When to feature a post

Feature your best 3–6 jobs. The homepage "Selected work" section shows up to 6 featured posts. If you feature more than 6, only the 6 most recent will appear on the homepage — the rest still show in Portfolio and Before & After.

Chapter 7

Deleting a post

Deleting a post is permanent — there's no undo. The post and all its images are removed from the database. If you only want to hide a post temporarily, switch Published off instead of deleting.

1

Open the post in the editor

Go to Posts, click the post's row to open it in the editor.

2

Click the trash icon

In the top right of the editor header (next to the X close button), click the small trash icon. You'll be asked to confirm.

3

Confirm deletion

Click OK in the confirm dialog. The post is deleted and the page reloads.

Recovering a deleted post

Deleted posts cannot be recovered from the admin panel. If you need to restore one, contact whoever manages your hosting — they can restore the database from a backup. To avoid this, prefer unpublishing over deleting.

Chapter 8

Replacing an existing image with a new one

There are two situations: replacing an image on a post you've already created (most common), and replacing a stock image in the homepage collage or hero. Both are covered below.

1

Open the post in the editor

Go to Posts, click the post's row. Scroll down to the Images section.

2

Remove the old image

Find the thumbnail of the image you want to replace. Click the small X button in its top-right corner. The thumbnail disappears from the grid.

3

Upload the new image

Drag the new photo into the upload box (or click to browse). It will appear as a new thumbnail at the end of the grid, tagged "G" (gallery).

4

Re-tag if needed

If the image you removed was tagged "before" or "after", click the B or A button on the new thumbnail to give it the same tag. The system only allows one before and one after per post, so the tag is automatically transferred.

5

Reorder to the right position

Use the left-arrow button on the new thumbnail to move it to the position the old image occupied. The first image in the grid becomes the cover image (shown on cards).

6

Save

Click Save. The old image is removed and the new one is live. (The old image file is still on your server but no longer linked — it can be cleaned up by your hosting provider.)

Replacing the homepage collage / hero images

The four images in the homepage hero (right-side collage) and the worker image on the old About section are currently loaded from Unsplash URLs hardcoded in src/components/site/Hero.tsx. To replace them with your own photos, ask whoever set up your site to swap the four src="https://images.unsplash.com/..." URLs with paths to your own images (uploaded to the public/ folder). This is a one-time change and is the only image swap that requires touching code.

Chapter 9

Managing testimonials

Testimonials are the client reviews that scroll across your homepage in the carousel. Each has a client name, an optional role/location label (e.g. "HDB Owner · Punggol"), a 1–5 star rating, and the review text. You can add, hide, or delete them anytime.

1

Open the Testimonials tab

Click Testimonials in the sidebar. You'll see a list of every testimonial on your site, with a small toggle showing Live / Hidden status.

2

Add a new testimonial

Click the "Add testimonial" button in the top right. Fill in the client's name, their role/location, pick a star rating (click the stars), and paste the review text. Toggle "Publish immediately" on. Click Save.

3

Hide or show a testimonial

Each testimonial has a small switch on its right side. Toggle it on to show the testimonial on the public site, off to hide it. Hidden testimonials are kept in the database — you can re-show them anytime.

4

Delete a testimonial

Click the small trash icon on the right side of the testimonial row. Confirm. The testimonial is permanently removed.

Chapter 10

Reading your analytics

The Analytics tab shows you how visitors are interacting with your site. All tracking is anonymous — no cookies, no personal data. The dashboard has four sections:

Site views
Total page loads across the whole site
Post views
How many times visitors opened a post detail modal
WhatsApp clicks
How many times a WhatsApp button was clicked
Avg dwell
Average seconds spent per page (lower is normal for quick visits)

Below the KPI cards, you'll see a 14-day line chart showing daily activity, and a bar chart of your top-6 most-viewed posts with their WhatsApp conversion. Use this to see which jobs attract the most interest — if a particular type of job (say, painting) gets lots of WhatsApp clicks, consider featuring more of those.

Chapter 11

Site Settings — change business name, phone, services

This is the most powerful tab in the admin panel. Everything visible on the public site is editable from here — no code changes required. The moment you click Save changes, the new values go live across the entire site.

What you can edit here

Business name, tagline, worker/contact name, phone, WhatsApp number, email, location, all showcase stats (years, jobs, clients, rating), hero headline & subtext, about title & body, and the full services list (add / edit / remove services).

1

Open Site Settings

Click Site Settings in the sidebar. The page loads with all your current values pre-filled in editable fields.

2

Edit any field

Click into any text box and change the value. Numbers (years, jobs, etc.) accept any whole number. The rating field accepts decimals (e.g. 4.9).

3

Edit the services list

Scroll to the Services List section. Each service is a card with a Label (e.g. "Plumbing"), an Icon (dropdown), a Key (lowercase slug), and a Description. Click any field to edit. Click "Add service" to add a new one. Click the trash icon on a service card to remove it.

4

Click Save changes

Hit the blue Save changes button at the top right (or the one at the bottom). A success toast appears, the page reloads, and your new values are live on the public site.

WhatsApp number format

The WhatsApp field must contain digits only, country code first, no + sign, no spaces. Example: 6591234567 (not +65 9123 4567). The phone field (for the "Call" button) can be formatted normally with spaces — that one is just for display.

Reset to defaults

If you ever want to undo all your changes and start over, click the Reset button. This restores the original demo values into the form (but does NOT save them — you still need to click Save to apply).

Chapter 12

Changing the hero headline & about text

The hero is the big section at the top of your homepage. It has a large headline (currently "Your home, expertly handled.") and a subtext paragraph below it. Both are editable from Site Settings.

Headline format tip

If your headline contains a comma, the text before the comma appears on line 1, and the text after the comma appears on line 2 (highlighted in the accent colour). Example: "Your home, expertly handled." renders as two lines. If you don't use a comma, the whole headline appears on one line.

The About section title and body are also editable from Site Settings. The current defaults are "Built on trust. Delivered with care." and a company-focused paragraph. Keep the about body to 2–4 sentences — longer paragraphs look cramped on mobile.

Chapter 13

Changing colours & dark mode

Your site has a warm amber colour scheme on a cream background, with a rich charcoal dark mode. Visitors can switch between light and dark using the moon/sun icon in the header. The colour palette is defined in CSS variables and is the only thing on the site that requires a code change to customise.

To change the primary colour from amber to something else (say, deep blue or forest green), ask whoever set up your site to edit the --primary CSS variable in src/app/globals.css. The change applies instantly — buttons, badges, the floating stat card, and all accents update together.

Why colours aren't in the Settings panel

Colours are tightly coupled with contrast ratios for accessibility and dark-mode support. A wrong colour choice can make text unreadable. For this reason, colour changes are a one-time setup decision, done by whoever built your site. All other content (text, images, services, stats) is fully self-serve from the Settings panel.

Chapter 14

Troubleshooting & FAQs

Common situations and how to handle them. If you can't find your answer here, contact whoever set up your hosting — they have access to the server logs and database directly.

I forgot my password

Passwords are hashed and cannot be recovered. Your hosting admin can reset it by running a one-line command in the project directory: bun run reset-password (or by editing the database directly). After reset, sign in with the new password and change it again from the admin panel.

My changes don't appear on the public site

The site caches pages for fast loading. After clicking Save, wait 2–3 seconds and refresh the public page (Cmd/Ctrl+R). If changes still don't show, your browser may be showing a cached version — try opening the site in an incognito/private window to confirm.

My uploaded image looks low quality

Images are auto-compressed to under 1.5 MB. If a photo looks blurry, it was probably very small to begin with. Try uploading a higher-resolution original — at least 1200×800 pixels works best. Phone photos are usually fine.

The before/after slider doesn't show

Check that the post has one image tagged "before" AND one tagged "after" (look for the red B badge and green A badge in the post editor's image grid). If only one is tagged, the post shows as a single photo — that's normal behaviour, not a bug.

WhatsApp button doesn't open a chat

Check that your WhatsApp number in Site Settings is digits only, country code first (e.g. 6591234567). If it has a + or spaces, the link will break. Save the settings and try again.
